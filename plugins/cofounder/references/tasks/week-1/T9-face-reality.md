# T9 — Face reality

Roughly an hour. Kept last and kept together on purpose: this is one act of honesty, not
three chores. Riskiest assumption, kill threshold, twenty people.

Everything above this task exists to make this task possible. Say that.

## Gating question

None.

## Steps

**Step 1 — list what this business dies without.** Read `concept.md`, `icp.md` and
`competitive-snapshot.md`. Rank assumptions by *impact if wrong* × *how little evidence
exists*. Pick the number one **with** the founder — do not pick it for them.

**Step 2 — state it as a falsifiable sentence.** One sentence, in `03-artifacts/
riskiest-assumption.md`. If it cannot be proved wrong by twenty conversations, it is not the
right sentence yet.

**Step 3 — the kill threshold, written BEFORE any testing.** Force the answer to:
*what result would kill this?*

> If fewer than 4 of 20 say they have this problem monthly and describe a real workaround,
> the assumption fails.

A number and a date. Do not let a threshold through that the founder could rationalise past
afterwards — that is the entire purpose of writing it first. If it is vague, tighten it out
loud, and say why you are pushing.

**Step 4 — the twenty people.** Build the list into `01-company/people.md`: twenty real,
named or findable people who plausibly have the problem.

Rules, stated before starting:
- one line of evidence per name — why you think *this* person has the problem;
- reachable within two weeks;
- friends and family only if they genuinely fit `icp.md`.

Pull from what is already in `people.md`, from the communities named in `icp.md` section 3,
and ask the founder for the rest. Twenty is hard and it is meant to be — if the list stalls
at nine, that is information about the segment, not about their effort. Say so, and put it
in `open-questions.md`.

## verify-M

- `03-artifacts/riskiest-assumption.md` exists, containing a falsifiable assumption sentence
  **and** a threshold with both a date and a number.
- `01-company/people.md` contains 20 rows, each with an evidence line.

Nineteen rows is not twenty. Say the count plainly and let the founder decide whether to
finish now or carry it into week 2 with the gap on the record.

## verify-J — rubric (always delivered, never blocks)

- **Would this actually kill the venture if false?** If the assumption could be wrong and
  the business survive, it is not the riskiest one. Name the one you think is riskier.
- **Is the threshold uncomfortable?** The test: *would a friend call this a real test?* A
  threshold met by two polite yeses is not a threshold.
- **Is it a test of the problem, not the pitch?** Twenty people saying "great idea" proves
  nothing. The threshold should turn on what they already do, pay for, or work around.
- **Do the twenty evidence lines say anything?** "Works in recruiting" is not evidence.
  "Posted in the r/recruiting thread about spreadsheet chaos in May" is.

## Week 1 close

When T9 verifies, all nine are done. Say it once, plainly, and show what they have:

> That's Week 1. You have: a co-founder that knows your business, your concept at three
> sizes, who it's for, what you're up against, a clean set of channels, and — the point of
> all of it — a dated bet you could lose, with twenty names against it.
>
> What you practised without noticing: instructions kept in files, work saved as artifacts
> rather than left in chat, sources cited, conflicts surfaced instead of smoothed over, and
> nothing marked done without a check. That discipline is the actual product of the week.
> The files are just the receipts.
>
> Week 2 is the twenty conversations.

Then report the time-actuals from `progress.md` — started and verified dates per task — and
ask one question: which task took longer than it should have? That answer is worth more than
the whole week's notes.
