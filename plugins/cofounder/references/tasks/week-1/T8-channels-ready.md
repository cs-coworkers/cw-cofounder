# T8 — Channels ready

Two things the founder needs in place before week 2: a name they could use, and a clean
account their co-founder can actually reach.

## Gating question — domain

> Do you already own a domain for this?

- **Yes** → log it in `03-artifacts/domains.md`, skip the shortlist entirely, move to the
  account. Record `domain-owned: yes — <domain> (asked T8, <date>)`.
- **No** → run the shortlist below.

## Part A — name candidates (skip if domain owned)

1. Generate 20 candidates across styles — descriptive, coined, metaphor — each with a
   one-line rationale, using `concept.md` and `icp.md`.
2. The founder picks five. Check apparent availability of the `.com` and the obvious social
   handles, and note close look-alikes.
3. Output a shortlist table: name, domain status, handles status, look-alike risk, gut note.

**Hard rule, retained from v1 and non-negotiable: NOTHING is purchased this week.** Say it
before generating, and end the section with exactly this, verbatim:

> Domain and handle availability is NOT trademark clearance. Do not commit brand assets
> (logo, signage, print) until a trademark knockout search in a later week. This is a
> candidate list, not a legal check.

If the founder wants to buy one now, do not argue twice: say the rule once, say why (the
knockout search comes first and can invalidate the name), and record their decision in
`open-questions.md` if they overrule it.

> **Success moment:** three names you wouldn't be embarrassed to say out loud, and you've
> spent nothing.

## Part B — a clean account

1. **A NEW Google account for the venture.** Not the personal one. A fresh empty business
   account is safe by construction: clean inbox, no personal mail, nobody else's data.
   Password manager and 2FA, per task 1 — if that was deferred, this is the moment to fix it
   (second and final offer).
   - A plain `gmail.com` address is correct right now. Moving to company-domain email after
     the domain is bought is cheap when planned. Do not stall waiting for a domain.
2. **Wire three connectors** to that new account: Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar. The
   authorisation is interactive and the founder is present for every grant — that is by
   design, not friction.
3. **Least privilege, three lines:** connect the clean account, never the personal one;
   grant the minimum the task needs; never connect a store holding other people's regulated
   data. None exists yet, which is exactly why the habit is cheap to form today.
4. **Find out what the connectors can actually do** — they differ, and some can create and
   read but not edit. Test each one, then save the real answer to `01-company/capabilities.md`:
   what each connector can do, what it cannot, and the date checked.

Be honest about the payoff: today, an empty inbox buys almost nothing. Drive is the bridge
for existing documents, Calendar starts mattering in week 2 when twenty conversations get
scheduled, and Gmail becomes the outreach channel. It gets wired now because the stakes are
zero now.

## verify-M

## Success moment

> Your co-founder can now reach your calendar, your files and your mail — on an account
> that has nothing personal in it. Task 8 of 9 done. One left, and it's the one that matters.
