# T6 — Who it's for

Roughly 45 minutes. Write `03-artifacts/icp.md`.

Introduce the term once — *ideal customer profile, the one specific kind of person you're
building for first* — then use plain words for the rest of the task.

## Gating question

None.

## The file

1. **One segment sentence, falsifiable:**
   *"Our first customer is `[role]` at `[company type/size]` who currently `[status-quo
   behaviour]` and feels `[pain]` when `[trigger moment]`."*
2. **Three observable markers** — things visible from the outside, without interviewing them.
3. **Where they already gather** — communities, tools, events.
4. **Who this is NOT** — the tempting adjacent segment being deliberately skipped, and why.

Section 3 is not filler: it feeds T9's twenty-person list directly. Say so when writing it.

## The challenge

If the segment sentence could describe more than roughly 10,000 organisations or a million
people, it is not a first customer — it is a market. Narrow it until it is testable.

Coach this, do not gate on it. Push, show the narrower version, and let the founder choose.

## verify-M

`03-artifacts/icp.md` exists and contains a single segment sentence.

## verify-J — rubric (always delivered, never blocks)

- **Falsifiable** — the sentence names at least one *observable* trait. "Cares about
  efficiency" is not observable. "Runs payroll for 20–100 people on spreadsheets" is.
- **Narrow** — it excludes most people. Read it back and name who it excludes; if the answer
  is "almost nobody", say so.
- **Findable on Thursday** — the real bar. Could the founder name three actual humans who
  fit, today, without research? Ask them to try. If they cannot, the segment is still a
  market, and week 2 will be twenty cold guesses.
- **The NOT section is specific** — a named adjacent segment, not "everyone else".

Coach until it bites. Do not tick a box on a sentence that would not survive contact with
week 2.

## Success moment

> That sentence is now the filter for every decision you make this year — who you build for,
> who you sell to, and who you say no to. Task 6 of 9 done.
